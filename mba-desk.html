02 / One-page memo · No charts

Problem framing

The real problem

MBA students, strategists, and consultants face 20–40 pages of business news each morning. The bottleneck is triage and judgment: which stories matter, to which business function, and what decisions they imply. Generic summaries compress prose but often leave that analytical work to the reader.

The false problems

  • “We need shorter summaries.” Brevity alone does not establish relevance or business implications.
  • “We need a bigger model.” Unclear scope and missing analytical structure are framing problems; a larger model is not automatically a remedy.
  • “We need every story.” Completeness can overwhelm the user. The supplied product history describes replacing a fixed dropdown with a numeric Top-N limit after a 32-page paper returned too many minor stories.
  • “We need image-based PDF reading.” Local text extraction can reduce payload and token use for PDFs with readable text. Scans remain a limitation.

The unknowns

  • Does relevance scoring agree with a human-labelled reference set?
  • How often does SWOT introduce claims unsupported by the source?
  • Do users read the output, save time, or change a decision? Usage evidence is TBD.
  • Do publishers’ terms permit the proposed processing? This has not been checked.
  • How reliably does extraction handle multi-column layouts and scanned pages?

These are product hypotheses and open questions, not validated research findings.

03 / Contract before capability

AI-worthiness & behaviour specification

Specification, not a desktop compliance claim.

The supplied document describes strict voice rejection but also reports a later move to prompt guidance. Current production enforcement remains TBD pending source and test evidence.

The never list

  1. Never introduce a fact, figure, or company claim absent from the supplied text.
  2. Never give buy, sell, or investment advice.
  3. Never analyse sports, crime, entertainment, or other non-business content.
  4. Never use narrator-style stock phrases in generated analytical output.
  5. Never present malformed output or an empty headline as a validated result.
  6. Never exceed the user’s Top-N article limit.
  7. Never send PDF text or credentials to an endpoint other than the configured Google Gemini service; keep reports on the user’s device.
  8. Never present partial or repaired output as validated without checking it again.

Why use AI here?

Business prose varies widely. Functional tagging, relevance assessment, and strategic interpretation require contextual judgment. AI is useful only when its output is bounded and easy to inspect. A confident but unsupported SWOT is worse than no analysis.

Keep deterministic: text extraction, schema checks, Top-N enforcement, local storage, and credential handling. Keep human review: source grounding, interpretation, and consequential decisions. Zod checks structure and constraints; it does not establish factual truth.

Intended

Return a headline, page, summaries, business impact, sector, allowed functional tags, relevance from 1–5, and 2–4 strategic-read bullets. Require four SWOT quadrants for relevance ≥2. Store validated reports locally.

Refusal

Skip non-business content. Decline investment advice. If extraction finds no text layer, stop and explain the limitation instead of guessing.

Failure

For schema or voice-check failure: retry once, then show an error and store nothing. For an API or network error: explain the reason and allow retry. These desktop paths still require verification.

Privacy boundary. Offline-first refers to local parsing and storage. Gemini analysis requires a network connection and sends extracted text to Google. The key is configured locally and used to authenticate that request; it is not a fully offline analysis pipeline.

04 / Available for Windows

Get MBA Desk

MBA Desk is an installable Windows desktop application built with Electron, Vite, and React. It extracts newspaper text locally, uses Google Gemini for business analysis, and stores reports in local SQLite.

Download Windows installer ↗Watch the walkthrough ↗

Start with the desktop app

  1. Open the downloads folder and download MBA Desk Setup.exe.
  2. Install and launch MBA Desk on Windows.
  3. Configure your Google Gemini API key in the app.
  4. Select a text-based newspaper PDF and choose your Top-N article limit.
  5. Generate a briefing and review functional tags, relevance scores, SWOT, and strategic reads.

An internet connection is required to generate analysis. Local parsing and report storage remain on your machine.
